WHY GPL, AND WHAT IS NOT

VGM Studio plays VGM and DRO files by linking chip emulator cores, and the
most accurate core for a given chip is often published under the GPL or the
LGPL (the Nuked family, the low-level-emulation cores). Accuracy was chosen
over license convenience, so the program as distributed is GPL-2.0-or-later.

Two of its components are deliberately *not* copyleft, so they can be reused
on their own:

  vgms-core   -- the VGM/DRO file model      MIT OR Apache-2.0
  vgms-synth  -- the playback engine and its
                permissively-sourced cores  MIT OR Apache-2.0

Copyleft cores are kept out of those two crates and live in provider crates
that only the application depends on. See licenses/README.md in the source
distribution for the full split, and crates/vgms-synth/PROVENANCE.md for the
per-core record of where each emulator came from and under what terms.

This build embeds the Nuked-OPL3 OPL2/OPL3 emulator (a Rust port of Nuke.YKT's
Nuked-OPL3), which is licensed under LGPL-2.1-or-later. The About dialog lists
every core compiled in, with its authors, license and upstream.

RetroWave OPL3 hardware output links the serialport crate, used under the
MPL-2.0. Its source: https://github.com/serialport/serialport-rs
