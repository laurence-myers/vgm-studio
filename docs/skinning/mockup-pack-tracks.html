Pack › Tracks: the row runs off the edge

Five per-row pads sit in a column that tells the layout it needs 200px and then draws ~345px. The title column takes “the rest” from the declared figure, so every row is laid out about 145px wider than the viewport: Edit… is cut in half at the window edge and the last 14px — the scrollbar channel — is painted over, which is why the handle has no puck. Four ways out, two subtle and two lateral, all drawn at 760px so the pressure is visible without a 1226px window.

Fix these whichever layout wins 2 lines

  • Declare the cluster's real width. Column::auto().at_least(200) is a promise the row does not keep. Measure the pads once and use Column::exact(w), or raise the floor to what is drawn. The remainder column then budgets from the truth and the table fits by construction.
  • Let the title clip, not push. remainder().clip(true) with a smaller floor (120) makes the long titles this pack is full of end in an ellipsis instead of shoving the pads off the edge — the title is the one cell that can afford to lose characters, because the row is a click target for the whole thing.

Neither changes the design. Both are worth doing even if one of the four below lands.

A — Icons, not words subtle

Open and Edit… become icon pads like their three neighbours: 345px → 174px, and the title gets the difference.

B — Actions on the row you are pointing at subtle

The column goes away entirely; the cluster floats over the row's right end on hover or selection. At rest the table is five short columns and a title, and cannot overflow.

C — Two lines per track lateral

Total, Loop and Peak stop being columns and become a muted second line. 170px of columns return to the title, and the numbers read as a sentence rather than a grid.

Costs: rows are twice as tall, so a 40-track pack scrolls twice as far, and the times no longer line up for comparison — which is half of why they are a table. Quick Edit moves into the double-click / status glyph, since only four pads fit. Risk: medium; it is a rewrite of the row, and it trades the pack's scanning job for its reading one.

D — List, then inspector lateral

The table keeps status, number and title and nothing else; everything about the selected track — its timings, its level, and every command with a real label — lives in a panel beside it. Width-proof at any window size.

Costs: one track at a time — reordering four tracks means four selections, where today the pads are on every row. Needs a real selection model in pack mode (there is only a preview row today) and a place for the panel to go when the window is narrow. Risk: high; it is a new view, not a fixed table.

The inline-icon direction. Three takes on the same idea: the per-row controls stop being keycaps and become glyphs in the data ink, sitting inside the columns that already exist. The trailing action column falls from 345px to 22px — or vanishes — which is more than the overflow needs, so the title gets everything back. What separates E, F and G is where reordering lives and how the two window-opening commands are reached.

E — Inline arrows, inline play, and a row menu as specified

▲▼ move to the left of the number, where the thing they reorder is; ▶ sits immediately before the title, where the thing it plays is; Open and Quick edit go behind ⋯ at the right end.

A glyph is a smaller target than a pad — 11px of ink wants ~18px of hit area, which the row height already affords. Risk: low. This is the layout that fits with the most room spare.

F — Drag to reorder, everything else as E less chrome

The pair of arrows becomes one grip: ⠇ — drag the row to where it goes. The arrows stay in the row menu for precision and for the keyboard.

G — One left gutter, nothing trailing hard right edge

Every control lives in a single 50px gutter before the number — ⋯ always, ▲▼ on the row under the pointer — so the row's right edge is pure data and the three time columns can sit flush against it.

Where I would land

  • E, plus the two lines under “fix these whichever layout wins”. It puts each control against the thing it acts on, ends the overflow with ~180px to spare, and keeps every command visible at rest — the objection that sinks B and G. The row menu is also where any future per-track command goes without touching the layout again.
  • F if reordering a long pack is the job. Dragging track 12 to the top is one gesture against eleven clicks; it is also the only option that needs new interaction code rather than new layout code.
  • A stays the fallback: the smallest possible change, all pads, no new idiom.
  • C if the row ever gains more data (chip, size, a second level figure) — once there are eight columns, no amount of declaring widths saves a 1226px window.
  • D is the right shape for a much larger pack tool, and the wrong amount of work for a clipped button. B and G hide reordering until you hover over it.
