Pack — screenshot treatments

The Screenshots section currently draws a bare row — file name, byte count, a Recompress pad — and then the raw egui::Image loose on the desktop with nothing around it. At the fixture's 2×2 pixels that is a red dot floating in space; at a real 320×200 title card it is an unframed rectangle with no edge, so a dark screenshot bleeds into the panel behind it.

Five treatments below, all built from chrome the skin engine already has. Every one uses the same stand-in image — a DOS title card — and the same facts: name, pixel size, byte size. They differ in what they optimise for: framing one image, comparing several, or reading the facts that decide whether a screenshot is submission-legal.

Why the facts matter. VGMRips wants a title screen at the game's native resolution. Byte count alone cannot tell you that; pixel dimensions can, and the section does not show them today. Several treatments below surface them, and S5 goes further and checks them.

S0 · Today

Name, bytes, a pad, then the bare image. No frame, no dimensions, and the caption row reads as a sentence rather than a record. The image has no boundary of its own, so a dark screenshot has no visible edge against the desk.

S1 · Sunken well

The cheapest fix, and the most consistent. Drop the existing row and image into the same sunken data well the track table and the waveform already use. The screenshot becomes displayed data, like everything else the app shows you, and the well's dark ground gives any image a hard edge. A checkerboard mat behind the image makes transparency and the image's own borders legible.

well = data_bg + bevel::paint_bevel(Sunken) — already used by the track table · no new primitive at all

S2 · Plate card

The screenshot as a mounted print. A raised plate card carries the image with a keyline frame, its name across the top and the actions along the bottom. It reads as an object you can act on rather than a region of the page — which suits a section whose whole job is "is this the right picture, and is it small enough". More chrome than S1 for one image; comes into its own with two or three.

card = plate_top/bottom gradient + plate_border (the same recipe as plate_panel, at widget scale) · frame = bevel_border keyline

S3 · Contact sheet

Built for more than one. Fixed-width thumbnail cells in a wrapping grid, each with its name and size beneath and a corner badge for state — amber BIG when a file is over the submission's comfortable size, green OK once recompressed. The selected cell wears the latch amber. Scales to a folder with six screenshots without becoming a mile of scrolling; the cost is that no single image is shown large, so it wants a click-to-enlarge.

cell = the S1 well at thumbnail scale · selection ring = latch_border/latch_bottom · badge = the meter roles

S4 · Slide mount

The skeuomorphic one. A 35mm slide mount: a thick surround, a hard-shadowed aperture the image sits behind, and a silkscreened data plate along the bottom edge. It is the most characterful option and the most obviously part of a hardware fascia — and the least practical, since the mount costs real vertical space per screenshot and says nothing S1 does not. Worth it only if the Screenshots section is meant to feel like a different kind of object from the rest of the app.

mount = a darker mix of the plate stops · aperture = bevel_border + an inset shadow · plate strip = the deck gradient at widget scale

S5 · Inspector (recommended)

The image beside the facts that decide it. The screenshot sits in an S1 well on the left; on the right, the record that actually answers "will VGMRips take this" — dimensions, aspect, colour depth, byte size — with anything doubtful in the warning amber and the actions beneath it. This is the only treatment that uses the section rather than just decorating it: today the app never tells you a screenshot's dimensions, and dimensions are the thing most likely to be wrong. The facts pane also gives the empty state somewhere sensible to live.

well = S1 · facts = the metadata form's label/value pair, in data_label/data_text · warnings reuse meter_mid, so they read like the checklist · a readiness item can be added per fact later
