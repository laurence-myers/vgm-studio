DRO Trimmer — tab strip styles

The Editor / Pack tabs are still bare ui.selectable_labels (app.rs), so the active one paints egui's visuals.selection.bg_fill: a flat square accent rectangle. That is generic egui — it shares no vocabulary with the plate, pads, wells or knobs around it, and the accent blue is a selection colour borrowed from the table, not a chrome colour. Eight replacements below, each built only from roles the skin engine already has.

Every strip is the real layout: a fascia plate band carrying the tabs, the panel seam groove the app already draws at its bottom, and the content well beneath. Colours are computed as the engine computes them — plate gradient, pad_caps, trough, the case's complementary data_text, and the shared hardware latch amber.

Disabled is shown for every variation (third strip in each row) — a real state once a pack is open but no track has been opened into the editor. All eight obey one rule, borrowed from the pan knob's greyed state: flatten toward the surface, dim the ink, never light. A disabled tab loses its dimensionality rather than gaining a new colour, so nothing has to be invented per case.

T0 · Today (the problem)

Square accent block, no bevel, no gradient, no border — it reads as a selected table row that wandered up onto the fascia. On the cream case it is worse: a saturated blue block on a warm ivory plate. Disabled has no treatment at all beyond egui's generic text fade.

Round one

T1 · Backlit pad tabs

The tabs become pads. Same rounded cap, border, glint and press language as every other button; the active tab is lit amber, exactly the engine's existing "pad lighting = active" rule. Cheapest to build and the most obviously part of the same machine — the risk is that a whole lit tab is a lot of amber, and amber already means "engaged" on the transport and channel pads.

unlit = pad_caps gradient + border + ink · lit = latch_top/bottom/border/ink · one call to bevel::toggle

Disabled: the cap sinks toward the plate and the ink fades into it — the pan knob's exact greyed recipe. It can never light.

T2 · Folder tabs, skeuomorphic

The active tab breaks the seam. Raised plate tongues with rounded shoulders: the active one is lifted (lighter gradient, lit top bevel) and its foot covers the panel groove, so it joins the surface that owns the content; the inactive one sits lower and darker with the groove running unbroken across it, reading as tucked behind. The most literal hardware/folder metaphor, and it reuses the seam groove the app already paints.

active = lighten(plate_top,.12)→plate_top, bevel_light top inset · inactive = darken(plate_bottom,…) · both framed plate_border · groove = bevel::groove_h

Disabled: the tongue flattens into the plate — no lit top edge, no shoulder shadow, border dimmed. It reads as moulded-in rather than a separate part.

T3 · Inset segmented selector, modern VST

A recessed track with a raised cap sliding in it. The pair sits in one sunken channel (the case's own trough, shadowed top, lit bottom); the active view is a raised pad cap inside it, the inactive one is just a label on the dark track. This is the segmented control every modern plugin uses, said in this app's materials — and it stays neutral, so amber keeps meaning "engaged" everywhere else.

track = trough + sunken bevel + plate_border · cap = pad_caps gradient/border/ink · inactive label = muted

Disabled: the label sinks toward the track it sits on, so the segment reads as an empty stretch of channel.

T4 · Silkscreen + indicator LED

No button at all. The view names are silkscreened straight onto the plate, letterspaced, with a small lens beside each: dark and dead when inactive, lit amber when live. The active name brightens from muted to the case's label. Quietest of the four and the most like real rack gear — the trade is that the hit target is invisible, so it leans entirely on the hover state to say "clickable".

lens unlit = darken(plate_bottom,.55) + inner shadow · lit = latch_top→latch_bottom (glow kept inside the lens) · labels = label / muted

Disabled: the lens goes fully dead — it loses its rim and its inner shadow, becoming a flat dark blank — and the silkscreen fades toward the plate, like unprinted panel.

Round two

T5 · Engraved plate

Machined, not mounted. Nothing is added to the fascia — the tabs are cut into it. Inactive views are debossed pockets (shadow on the top lip, highlight on the bottom) with engraved labels; the active view is a polished raised boss. Pure light and shadow, no second colour at all, so it is the only variation that is guaranteed to sit correctly on any future case.

pocket = darken(plate_*) + inset shadow / bottom highlight · boss = lighten(plate_top,.14)→plate_top + bevel_light top · engraved label = 1px light text-shadow below the glyphs

Disabled: the engraving gets shallow — the pocket keeps its shape but loses most of its shadow depth, so it reads as a blank milled recess with no label worth reading.

T6 · Display tabs

Navigation moves into the screen. The tabs sit in a dark well like the readout wells, and the active label burns in the case's complementary data_text — gold on navy, tracker yellow on teal, cyan on cream and rust. It borrows the one colour that is already a signature of this skin engine, and it makes the tab strip read as part of the instrument's display rather than its casework.

well = data_bg + sunken bevel · active cell = data_stripe, label data_text · inactive label = data_label

Disabled: the label falls toward data_bg — a display segment that simply is not being driven, which is exactly what a dark LCD cell looks like.

T7 · Lit rail, modern VST

One bar carries the state. Flat labels on the plate with a recessed rail running beneath them; the active tab lights its length of that rail in data_text and brightens its label. The most restrained and the most scalable — it stays legible at three, four or six views where every raised-cap variation starts to crowd — and the recessed rail keeps it from looking like flat web chrome dropped onto a hardware fascia.

rail channel = darken(plate_bottom,.45) inset · lit length = data_text · labels = label / muted

Disabled: the rail stays dark under it and the label fades into the plate; with no cap or box to flatten, the absence of light is the disabled state.

T8 · Rocker selector

A real two-position switch. A recessed housing split by a pivot line: the chosen side is pressed in (darkened, shadowed along its top lip) and the other stands proud. It inverts the usual "active = raised" convention on purpose, because that is how a rocker physically works, and it is the most tactile thing on the sheet. The honest caveat: it only makes sense for exactly two views — a third would need a different control entirely.

housing = darken(plate_bottom,.35) + plate_border · pressed = darkened plate + top inset shadow · proud = lighten(plate_top,.10) + bevel_light top

Disabled: the half goes flat — neither pressed nor proud — so the rocker reads as seized, with a dimmed label.
