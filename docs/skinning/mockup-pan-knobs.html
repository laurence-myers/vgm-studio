DRO Trimmer — pan-knob styles

Five treatments for the pan / spread knobs, rebuilt from the pad grammar so the knob follows the selected pad surface (theme default, or a Light / Dark / Grey / Tint override) exactly as the digit pads under it do. Every colour below is computed the way the engine computes it: cap gradient, border and ink from palette::pad_caps, the deck from deck_stops, the lit amber from the shared hardware latch ramp.

Each strip shows the knob at hard-L · L50 · C · R50 · hard-R, the disabled state, the digit pads it must sit over (lit + unlit), and an actual-size 20 px trio.

K0 · Today (for reference)

The current painter uses the FT2 bevel-button recipe: button_face cap, two-tone bevel ring, data_text pointer. Those are case roles, so the knob ignores the pad surface entirely — note how it stays a navy-grey cap on row two even though the pads went Dark. That is the clash being fixed.

K1 · Round keycap

The pad recipe bent around a circle. Cap gradient top→bottom, 1 px pad border, glint arc along the upper edge, shade arc along the lower, pointer + hub in pad ink. The safest of the five: reads instantly as the same family as the digit pad below it.

cap = caps.top→caps.bottom · border = caps.border · glint/shade = white/black alpha (as paint_pad) · pointer + hub = caps.ink

K2 · Square pad knob

Literally a pad that rotates its glyph. Same rounded-square footprint, corner radius, glint line and border as the digit pad — the pan row and toggle row become one uninterrupted grid of caps. Risk: it reads as a button until the pointer is noticed; the hub dot and 270° sweep carry the "knob" affordance.

identical to paint_pad (rect, radius 3, inset glint/shade) + pointer/hub in caps.ink

K3 · Backlit lamp pointer

The value is a lit lamp. Neutral pad cap; the pointer is a small amber capsule in the hardware latch colours — the same "lamp behind the key" the pads light when active. Disabled, the lamp goes out to plain ink. Strong at a glance; the trade is that amber stops meaning only "latched/active" and also means "value".

cap as K1 · lamp = latch_top/latch_bottom with latch_border outline · unlit/disabled lamp = caps.ink

K4 · Cap on skirt, silkscreen ticks

The most hardware of the five. A darker skirt ring under a smaller domed cap, with L·C·R tick marks silkscreened on the deck around it. The pointer runs cap-to-skirt in pad ink. Costs 3–4 px of breathing room per side for the ticks, so the pan rows get slightly taller.

skirt = darken(caps.bottom, .25) · cap = caps gradient · ticks = deck silkscreen (luma-picked, as deck_ink) · pointer = caps.ink

K5 · Amber value arc

The value is a lit arc. Flat pad cap with a recessed 270° track; the swept portion from centre to the position fills with latch amber, ending in an ink dot. Bipolar from 12 o'clock, so it draws the pan and the spread knob with one rule and centre reads as "dark track, no light". Thinnest indicator at actual size — check the 20 px strip.

cap as K1 · track = black-alpha groove · lit arc = latch_bottom (tip latch_top) · position dot = caps.ink

Round two — full-radius arc, no dots

Six variations on one idea: the value is a 270° arc at the full knob radius, lit from 12 o'clock toward the position, with no centre hub and no position dot. At unity (centre) nothing is lit — the knob is a plain cap on a dark track, which is exactly the pads' "unlit at rest" language. Orientation at unity comes from the track itself (its two ends mark hard-L/hard-R).

K6 · Solid channel arc

The straight version. A recessed channel ring around the rim; the lit span is a solid amber fill with a hot tip. The inner cap keeps the pad gradient + glint. The baseline the other five riff on.

cap = caps gradient · channel = black-alpha groove · fill = latch_bottom, tip latch_top

K7 · Fine LED ring

An LED display bent into a ring. 27 discrete 10° segments with gaps; lit segments amber, the tip segment hot, unlit segments a faint recess so the sweep range always reads. The most "hardware display" of the set — it pairs naturally with a future LED volume readout.

segments lit = latch_bottom / tip latch_top · unlit = black-alpha · cap = caps gradient

K8 · Chunky LED blocks

The hard-pixel tracker take. Nine fat 30° blocks — DOS-era resolution, like a VU meter wrapped around the cap. Coarser value read (each block ≈ 22% pan), but the chunky steps stay legible at 20 px where fine segments blur together.

as K7, at 9 × 30° resolution

K9 · Skeuomorphic glow

A real lamp in a real channel. Domed cap (radial highlight, rim shadow) sunk into a dark channel; the lit span blooms with a soft amber glow outside its stroke. The one deliberate break from the "no outer glow" pad rule — the glow lives inside the knob's own channel, not outside the widget rect.

dome = radial caps.top→bottom · channel = black-alpha · glow = blurred latch_top under a latch_bottom core

K10 · Modern VST, hairline

Serum/Vital language, tracker colours. A flat single-colour cap floating inside a thin round-capped arc, with a clear moat between them. No glint, no bevel — the flattest thing on the sheet, so it reads slightly apart from the pads; the shared cap colour and amber are what tie it in.

cap = flat lerp(caps.top, caps.bottom, .5) · track = ink-alpha hairline · lit = latch_bottom→latch_top toward the tip

K11 · Modern VST, sector fill

The knob is the display. No separate track: the lit value is a filled annular sector carved out of the cap itself, edge-to-edge — the arc literally occupies the full radius. Boldest value read of the six; at unity it is indistinguishable from a plain round pad, which is either the point or the problem.

disc = caps gradient · sector = latch_bottom→latch_top fill with latch_border keyline

K12 · Skeuomorph, unplugged

K9 without the lamp. Same domed cap sunk in the same dark channel, but the lit span is a solid flat amber stroke — no bloom, no hot core. This honours the pads' "no outer glow" rule to the letter: the arc reads as a painted indicator in the channel rather than a light source.

body as K9 · fill = solid latch_bottom, no filter

K13 · Skeuomorph, heat ramp

Same body; the colour carries the magnitude. The arc runs from a dark ember at 12 o'clock toward a tip whose heat tracks how far the pan is from unity — a small pan is a short dim ember, hard L/R runs the full ramp up to bright latch amber. You can read "how wide" from colour temperature alone, before reading arc length. Mirrors the VU meter's cold-to-hot habit.

body as K9 · fill = ramp latch_border → latch_bottom → latch_top, tip heat scaled by |pan − centre|
